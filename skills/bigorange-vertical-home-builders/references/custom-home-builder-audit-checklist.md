# Custom home builder marketing audit checklist

One-line summary: 52 yes/no questions across six areas - run every item against the actual live site and CRM before it fills any deck slide, never as a hypothetical.

Score each item yes / no / not applicable. A "no" is a finding, not a failure - write it down plainly and move on.

## Website and conversion (10)

1. Does the homepage name the kind of builder (custom, semi-custom, design-build) within the first screen? Why it matters: buyers self-sort fast; a vague homepage funds confusion downstream (BLOG-02).
2. Is the service area named specifically, not just implied? Why it matters: "do you work in my area" is one of the five core buyer jobs (BLOG-01).
3. Does every priority landing page have one visible, primary call to action? Why it matters: five competing paths on a page turns paid traffic into wasted spend (BLOG-02).
4. Is the site fully usable on a phone, with no horizontal overflow or broken forms? Why it matters: most buyer research happens off-hours on personal devices (NAHB/CallRail 2026).
5. Does the consultation/contact form explain what happens after submission (who responds, what timeframe)? Why it matters: a vague "Contact us" leaves the buyer guessing and increases poor-fit inquiries (BLOG-01, item 10).
6. Is there a dedicated process page explaining the major stages (fit, feasibility, design coordination, scope alignment, planning, construction communication, handoff)? Why it matters: proof of judgment matters more than a perfect timeline (BLOG-01, item 3).
7. Are there current people/role pages with real photography and plain descriptions of who does what? Why it matters: custom building is a long relationship; a title alone isn't proof of one (BLOG-01, item 4).
8. Does at least one page tell a full project decision story (goal, constraint, choice, result) rather than just showing a gallery? Why it matters: a gallery says "we built this"; a decision story says "here is how we think" (BLOG-01, item 1).
9. Is there a first-conversation planning guide or checklist visible without demanding a form fill first? Why it matters: a visible article earns trust; hiding the useful part behind a gate erodes it (BLOG-01, item 2).
10. Does the site avoid unsupported guarantees about outcomes, cost, or timeline? Why it matters: no responsible builder or agency can promise magic; the voice rule is to say what will be built, checked, or measured (brand-voice.md).

## Proof and permissions (8)

11. Is there a written record of which homeowners/projects are cleared for public use? Why it matters: names, addresses, budgets, and private details must stay protected unless explicitly cleared (BLOG-01, item 1).
12. Are reviews quoted with exact approved language and a named source? Why it matters: paraphrased or unattributed reviews are harder to trust and easier to challenge (BLOG-01, item 9).
13. Are reviews placed next to the relevant service or project instead of isolated on one testimonials page? Why it matters: context makes proof credible; isolation makes it generic (BLOG-01, item 9).
14. Is there a documented image-permission chain for featured and social images? Why it matters: public use requires image-permission review before release (growth-plan audit, section 05).
15. Does published proof avoid turning one homeowner's experience into a universal claim? Why it matters: overgeneralizing a single result is a credibility risk and a voice-rule violation (BLOG-01, item 9; brand-voice.md rule 8).
16. Is there a monthly (or regular) proof-review pass checking active service areas, current people, and project permissions? Why it matters: stale proof - an ex-employee still listed, a project no longer approved - quietly erodes trust (BLOG-01, item 11).
17. Are awards, certifications, and credentials current and sourced (not expired or unverifiable)? Why it matters: proof that can't be verified isn't proof.
18. Is FAQ or BlogPosting structured data limited to claims that match the visible page content? Why it matters: schema should describe the page accurately; it cannot manufacture a ranking or rich-result guarantee (growth-plan audit, section 05).

## Local presence and Google Business Profile (8)

19. Does the Google Business Profile have complete, accurate business information (category, hours, service area, attributes)? Why it matters: Google says complete information helps it understand and evaluate the business for relevance (support.google.com/business/answer/7091).
20. Is the business profile verified? Why it matters: verification shows Google the listing is authorized and legitimate.
21. Does the team actively respond to reviews? Why it matters: Google names review response as part of demonstrating engagement, which feeds prominence.
22. Are photos and videos on the profile current and representative of real work? Why it matters: prominence is partly built from how well-known and well-documented a business is.
23. Does each real service area have its own dedicated market page (not a templated city-name swap)? Why it matters: relevance depends on genuinely matching what someone is searching for, not just naming a place (BLOG-01, item 5).
24. Do market pages name specific communities, project types that fit, and locally relevant planning considerations? Why it matters: a generic page with a swapped city name doesn't prove geographic fit (BLOG-01, item 5).
25. Is there any claim, on-site or in outreach, implying a paid or requested better local ranking? Why it matters: Google explicitly states there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking - such a claim is both false and a liability (support.google.com/business/answer/7091).
26. Are NAP (name, address, phone) details consistent across the website, GBP, and any directory listings? Why it matters: inconsistency undermines the completeness signal Google uses for relevance.

## Search foundation (10)

27. Is the builder hub / primary commercial page crawlable and indexable (no unintended noindex or nofollow)? Why it matters: BigOrange's own September 2026 audit found an unintended nofollow directive on a related page - this class of defect silently kills visibility (growth-plan audit, section 02).
28. Does every priority page have exactly one H1? Why it matters: the same audit found a page with four H1 elements, which confuses both crawlers and readers (growth-plan audit, section 02).
29. Are metadata fields (title, meta description) unique per page, with no copy-paste duplication? Why it matters: duplicated or generic metadata weakens both click-through and topical clarity.
30. Is the XML sitemap complete and does it match what's actually publishable? Why it matters: the same audit found 21 published pages missing from the sitemap - sitemap inclusion isn't proof of indexing, but exclusion is a real gap (growth-plan audit, sections 02 and 07).
31. Does each page target one primary query family without competing against a sibling page (cannibalization check)? Why it matters: two pages chasing the same query family split authority instead of building it (growth-plan audit, section 06).
32. Is there a direct, scannable answer near the top of key informational pages? Why it matters: Google's people-first guidance rewards content that helps a reader feel they learned enough to reach their goal quickly (developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).
33. Does content avoid publishing purely to hit a word count or content-calendar quota? Why it matters: Google explicitly warns against content produced primarily to manipulate rankings rather than serve a real audience.
34. Is mobile page performance (Core Web Vitals / LCP) acceptable on priority pages? Why it matters: the same audit measured a performance score of 32 with LCP near 36 seconds on a builder-hub-class page - a real, not hypothetical, failure mode.
35. Do internal links connect supporting content back to the commercial hub in a clearly labeled way? Why it matters: the authority model is one commercial parent with supporting educational assets - orphaned or unlinked content doesn't reinforce the hub (growth-plan audit, section 06).
36. Is meaningful alt text present on images that convey information (and correctly empty on purely decorative ones)? Why it matters: this is both an accessibility requirement and a factual audit finding from BigOrange's own site review (growth-plan audit, section 02).

## Follow-up and CRM (8)

37. Is there an immediate, clearly-labeled (not disguised as personal) acknowledgment sent on every new inquiry? Why it matters: a fake-personal auto-reply damages trust faster than an honest automated one (BLOG-03, item 1).
38. Are leads routed by market and project type to a named owner? Why it matters: automation should organize facts; a person still has to decide fit (BLOG-03, item 2).
39. Is there a documented response-time expectation, and is it actually being met? Why it matters: only 41% of teams respond immediately to after-hours inquiries even though responsiveness now ties price as the top factor in buyer choice (NAHB/CallRail, 2026-01-30).
40. Does the nurture sequence stop or change when someone books, replies, or opts out? Why it matters: automation that keeps talking after the buyer has raised a hand becomes "a polite spam machine" (BLOG-03, item 5).
41. Are long-horizon leads segmented by real stage (researching, preparing, evaluating, conversation active) rather than one blanket sequence? Why it matters: the same message to a not-ready researcher and an active buyer wastes both (BLOG-03, item 5).
42. Does sales have a simple, structured way to record inquiry outcome (accepted, nurture, disqualified, duplicate, unreachable)? Why it matters: without this, marketing can report submissions but never explain whether the work created better-fit conversations (BLOG-03, item 6; BLOG-02).
43. Is the source, campaign, and page context preserved when a lead is routed? Why it matters: losing this breaks the measurement chain from discovery through to sales outcome (BLOG-03, item 2).
44. Has every active automation been documented with its seven controls (trigger, audience, permission, content, owner, stop condition, measure)? Why it matters: a workflow isn't live-ready just because the toggle says active (BLOG-03, "Build every workflow with seven controls").

## Measurement (8)

45. Does reporting distinguish delivery, traffic quality, meaningful action, inquiry fit, sales acceptance, and business outcome as separate stages? Why it matters: skipping straight from clicks to revenue claims a connection that was never verified (BLOG-02, "Use a measurement ladder").
46. Is there a documented, agreed definition of a "qualified inquiry" (geography, project type, planning horizon, decision role, investment readiness)? Why it matters: a form submission is an action, not automatically a qualified opportunity (BLOG-02, "Define a qualified inquiry before launch").
47. Do website events, CRM contacts, and sales outcomes use the same definitions and actually reconcile? Why it matters: conversion reporting should be labeled "pending validation" until they do - never presented as settled (growth-plan audit, section 08).
48. Is there a weekly check for indexing issues, broken links, form delivery failures, and reporting anomalies? Why it matters: this is the minimum cadence the growth-plan audit recommends to catch failures before they compound (growth-plan audit, section 08).
49. Is there a monthly review of query-to-page ownership, content decay, and the qualified-inquiry backlog? Why it matters: same source, monthly cadence - this is where cannibalization and stale content get caught.
50. Is there a quarterly technical crawl, schema-coverage check, and internal-link review? Why it matters: same source, quarterly cadence - structural issues compound if left to the monthly cycle alone.
51. Does any reported number avoid presenting an estimate as a guarantee? Why it matters: no responsible party can guarantee a ranking, AI citation, lead count, or signed project (BLOG-05).
52. Is there a named owner for each report who can explain what was verified versus what remains an assumption? Why it matters: "ask what was verified, what remains an assumption, and which source owns each number" is the standard the growth-plan audit holds itself to (growth-plan audit, section 04, "Ask providers these ten questions").
