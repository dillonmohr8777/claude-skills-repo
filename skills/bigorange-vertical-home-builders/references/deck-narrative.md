# Custom home builder pitch deck - slide-by-slide narrative

One-line summary: the arc moves from the buyer's real hesitation, to what's broken, to proof BigOrange already has, to a bounded plan the builder can say yes to - never a promise the work can't back up.

How to use this: `bigorange-client-decks` recipes/industry-pitch.json reads this file for slide order, slot names, and copy guidance, and reads `industry-brief.md` for the facts that fill each slot. Every slide below names which brief section supplies its content. Slides marked **[VERIFIED PROOF REQUIRED]** must not ship with placeholder or invented client evidence - see the fail-closed rule in SKILL.md.

Voice while writing every slide: warm, plain, one playful line per section at most, no em dashes, no unlock/elevate/empower/ecosystem/synergy/transformative/future-ready/robust. Lead with the reader's problem, not BigOrange.

## Slide 1 - Title

- **slot: title** - "[Builder Name] × BigOrange" or "A Marketing Plan for [Builder Name]"
- **slot: kicker** - "Custom home builder marketing, built around how your buyers actually decide"
- **slot: proof** - BigOrange award/credential line (StoryBrand Certified, 50+ 5-star Google reviews, Best in Cincy 2023, Expertise Cincinnati 2025) - pull verbatim from industry-brief.md "Who the buyer is" sources, cite bigorange.marketing hub page.
- Copy guidance: no tagline invention. Use BigOrange's own phrasing from the hub page ("Get more leads with digital marketing for residential builders").
- Brief field: industry-brief.md sources #1.

## Slide 2 - The buyer's real hesitation

- **slot: title** - "Your buyer isn't shopping price first. They're shopping trust."
- **slot: body** - Name the five buyer jobs (can you build what I want / do you work in my area / what will this be like / can I trust your team / what should I do next).
- **slot: stat** - Responsiveness tied with price as the #1 factor (67%, NAHB/CallRail 2026-01-30); only 41% respond immediately after hours.
- Copy guidance: one playful line max, e.g. "Buyers decide slowly, anxiously, and usually at 9pm on a Sunday."
- Brief field: industry-brief.md "Their customer's journey" section.

## Slide 3 - The market right now

- **slot: title** - "Buyers have options. That raises the bar on proof."
- **slot: stat** - 607,000 SAAR new single-family sales, July 2026; 9.6 months of supply (Census/HUD, https://www.census.gov/construction/nrs/pdf/newressales.pdf).
- **slot: stat 2** - Custom building starts up 3% YoY in Q1 2026 to 36,000, holding ~20% of single-family starts (NAHB/Eye On Housing, 2026-05-25).
- **slot: body** - Frame: elevated supply means buyers can compare; custom's relative resilience is the case for leaning into proof, not discounting.
- **[VERIFIED PROOF REQUIRED]** - these are national stats; never imply they're the builder's local market without a locally sourced figure.
- Brief field: industry-brief.md "Seasonality and market context."

## Slide 4 - What's usually broken (diagnostic, not accusation)

- **slot: title** - "Here's where most builder marketing actually breaks"
- **slot: body** - Five-item list: site/destination, proof, local presence, follow-up, measurement (from industry-brief.md "What is usually broken").
- Copy guidance: phrase as a mirror, not an insult - "See if any of these sound familiar" rather than "your site is bad."
- Brief field: industry-brief.md "What is usually broken."

- Brief field: BLOG-05 "Use a bounded first engagement"; industry-brief.md "Compliance and permission cautions" (no-guarantee framing must survive onto this slide too).

## Notes for the recipe

- Slides 5 and 12 are the two hard-gated **[VERIFIED PROOF REQUIRED]** slides - the recipe should refuse to auto-fill them from generic brief content and should flag them for manual proof insertion.
- Every stat slide (2, 3, 9, 11) must carry its source and date in a visible footer, not just in speaker notes - matches the brand-voice rule "state the source, date, number, and gap when presenting proof."
- If the prospect is not yet a client (cold pitch), drop slide 5 entirely rather than showing a hypothetical audit - replace with an offer to run one.
