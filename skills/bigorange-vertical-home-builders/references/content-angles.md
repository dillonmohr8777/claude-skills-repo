# Content angles - custom home builders

One-line summary: 20 angles grouped by the five buyer jobs from the brief, each with a one-line BigOrange-voice hook and the specific proof it needs before it can ship.

Every angle needs real, approved evidence before publication - a headline alone is not a deliverable. Mark any angle "needs proof" that this builder doesn't yet have on hand.

## Buyer job: "Can you build what I want?" (taste and judgment)

1. **Blog** - "How We Decide: [material/window/layout] tradeoffs on a real project." Hook: "Buyers see the finished room. They rarely see the argument that got you there." Proof needed: one approved project decision, named designer/builder willing to explain the call.
2. **Video** - 90-second walkthrough of one finished project, narrated by the builder explaining one hard decision. Hook: "Not a tour. The one call that almost went the other way." Proof needed: homeowner permission, approved footage.
3. **Email** - "Before you call, see how we think" - send a decision story to a new inquiry before the first conversation. Hook: "Here's a project like yours, and the call we made on it." Proof needed: an existing decision story matched to the lead's project type.
4. **Social** - carousel: goal → constraint → choice → result, one project per post. Hook: "The room you're picturing usually starts with a problem, not a Pinterest board." Proof needed: approved photos with captions that explain, not just show.

## Buyer job: "Do you work in my area?" (geographic fit)

5. **Blog** - one market page per real service area, not templated copy. Hook: "We didn't swap the city name. We wrote about your neighborhood." Proof needed: real local project examples, named communities.
6. **Social** - "spotted in [neighborhood]" post when a project completes in a new area. Hook: "Another one wrapped a few blocks from [landmark]." Proof needed: homeowner and photo permission, accurate location.
7. **Email** - geo-targeted nurture segment: send the relevant market page, not the generic hub, to leads by service area. Hook: none needed - utility content, keep it plain.
8. **Video** - a 60-second "why we work in [market]" clip from the builder, naming what's different about building there (terrain, permitting, HOA patterns). Hook: "[Market] has its own rules. Here's what we've learned building here." Proof needed: locally specific, factually checked planning notes.

## Buyer job: "What will this be like?" (process and communication)

9. **Blog** - the honest process guide: fit, feasibility, design coordination, scope alignment, planning, construction communication, handoff. Hook: "Not a perfect timeline. Proof we know how to guide the uncertain parts." Proof needed: current, accurate internal process - this has to match what actually happens.
10. **Downloadable** - "What to bring to your first conversation" checklist. Hook: "You're not too early, and you don't need a binder." Proof needed: none beyond accurate internal guidance - low proof bar, high usefulness bar.
11. **Email sequence** - 5-part planning-cycle nurture: first conversation prep, site questions, design relationships, investment variables, project examples. Hook: each message needs its own reason to exist, not "just checking in." Proof needed: existing project examples relevant to the segment.
12. **Video** - a short "what happens after you sign" explainer walking through the handoff and communication cadence. Hook: "The part nobody markets: what Tuesday actually looks like." Proof needed: accurate, current operational detail.

## Buyer job: "Can I trust your team?" (relationship fit)

13. **Blog** - people and role pages: who answers questions, who leads design, who manages the site. Hook: "A title isn't a relationship. Here's who you'll actually talk to." Proof needed: current photography, accurate role descriptions, team consent.
14. **Social** - "meet the team" series, one person per post, told through what they actually do for a client. Hook: "Not a headshot wall. The person who'll return your calls." Proof needed: team permission and current photos.
15. **Video** - short interview clips with the builder and lead designer/PM, answering one buyer-worry question each. Hook: "We asked our own team the questions you're too polite to ask." Proof needed: honest, unscripted-feeling answers - no manufactured confidence.
16. **Email** - reviews placed beside the relevant service or project, with attribution and date, not batched onto one testimonials page. Hook: "One homeowner's story is one homeowner's story - here's whose it is." Proof needed: exact approved review language, named source.

## Buyer job: "What should I do next?" (helpfulness before the sale)

17. **Blog** - answer one expensive buyer question a month (land-before-builder, when to involve an architect, what changes investment most, how selections are handled, planning timeline). Hook: "The question that quietly kills a lot of first calls - answered straight." Proof needed: accurate, currently-true process and pricing-variable language, reviewed by the builder.
18. **Downloadable** - planning checklist gated lightly (visible summary, downloadable detail) rather than hidden entirely behind a form. Hook: "The useful part shouldn't cost you your email first." Proof needed: none - utility content.
19. **Email** - a monthly "proof review" recap sent internally, then repurposed as a public "here's what changed" note when something material updates (new market, new team member, updated process). Hook: "We keep our own site honest - here's this month's update." Proof needed: an actual internal proof-review pass has to have happened (BLOG-01, item 11).
20. **Social** - qualified-next-step post explaining who the consultation is for, what happens after the form, and who responds. Hook: "Not a funnel ambush. Here's exactly what happens after you hit submit." Proof needed: an accurate, current description of the real intake process.

## Notes for use

- Angles 9, 11, 17, and 20 depend on internal process facts the builder must supply and approve - never invent a process, timeline, or investment variable to fill the angle.
- Angles 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 13, 14, 15, and 16 depend on homeowner or team permission - confirm before drafting, not after.
- Pair each shipped angle with the measurement ladder in `industry-brief.md` - track it past the click, not just at publish.
