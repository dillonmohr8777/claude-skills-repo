# MSP / IT services industry brief

One line: MSPs know how to fix a server at 2am, most cannot explain in one sentence why a prospect should switch to them, and that sentence is the whole marketing problem.

## Who the buyer is

The MSP itself is our buyer, not the MSP's clients, we sell to the person who owns or runs the shop.

- **Owner-operator / founder** at a 5-50 employee MSP. Usually came up on the technical side (a former sysadmin or break-fix tech who started billing monthly). Wears sales, marketing, and delivery hats at once. This is the primary contact for BigOrange's MSP work, see the "off your plate" framing in the brand voice.
- **VP of Sales / Business Development** at larger MSPs (50+ employees), often hired once the founder can no longer personally close every deal.
- **Marketing coordinator or fractional marketer**, if one exists at all, BigOrange's own hub page frames this directly: "Running a great MSP is hard enough. Marketing IT shouldn't feel like a mystery" (fetched 2026-09-02). Most MSPs under 25 employees have nobody in this seat.

What they are worried about, in their words: keeping current clients happy while trying to find new ones, a website that gets traffic but "never seems to generate actual leads" (BigOrange hub page, fetched 2026-09-02), and increasingly, whether ChatGPT and AI answers even mention them at all.

## Their customer's journey (who the MSP sells to, and how those buyers research)

The MSP's own buyer is usually an office manager, controller, or IT director at a 10-200 employee company who has been burned by a bad vendor before and is quietly Googling alternatives.

- B2B buyers overall report preferring to do research without a sales rep: Gartner's 2025 survey (632 B2B buyers) found 61% prefer a rep-free buying experience, and 73% actively avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach (source: Gartner survey via Demand Gen Report, published 2025-07-01, survey dated 2025-06-25; fetched 2026-09-02). Read for MSPs: the prospect has already formed an opinion of you from your website and reviews before anyone picks up a phone.
- The MSP buying decision is trust-heavy and infrequent, most companies only shop MSPs once every few years, so search intent is often triggered by an incident (a breach, an outage, a bad renewal) rather than steady demand.
- Proof beats pitch. A prospect comparing three MSPs is reading reviews, checking how long the company has existed, and looking for a specific story that resembles their own problem, not a services list.

## What is usually broken

1. **Website**, built years ago by whoever was cheapest, full of jargon (managed services, proactive monitoring, ecosystem language) that reads the same on every MSP site in the country. Nothing that differentiates.
2. **Proof**, no client logos, no named case studies, no reviews surfaced above the fold. BigOrange's own hub page leans on named proof (Internos doubled their SEO; a Seattle MSP saw website traffic jump 50 percent and leads triple in four months, fetched 2026-09-02) precisely because generic MSP sites have none of this.
3. **Positioning**, "we do IT so you don't have to" said fifty different ways. No specific niche, no specific pain named.
4. **Lead capture**, a contact form and a phone number, no path for someone who is not ready to talk yet (no guide, no assessment, no low-commitment next step).
5. **Sales handoff**, leads that do come in go into an inbox, not a CRM, so nothing gets nurtured and nothing gets measured.
6. **Measurement**, no attribution. The owner cannot say which channel produced the last five signed clients.

## Objections and answers

- **"We already tried SEO and it didn't work."** Ask what was actually measured and for how long, BigOrange's own timeline guidance is early movement within the first month, with consistent and compounding results around six months out, because domain authority gains carry a 60-90 day lag (BigOrange hub page, fetched 2026-09-02). A three-month test is not a fair test.
- **"Our clients don't find us on Google, they find us through referrals."** True today, but referrals do not scale past a certain size, and the next generation of buyers is checking online anyway before they'll take a referral seriously.
- **"We don't have time to be a marketing department."** That is the pitch, not the objection, the work should come off their plate, not add to it.
- **"AI search feels like a fad."** Reasonable skepticism, answer with what is actually checkable now: whether the MSP's name, service area, and specialty show up when someone asks ChatGPT or Google's AI overview a buying question, testable in a five-minute demo, not a promise.

## Proof patterns that work

- Named, dated client results with a specific number and a specific timeframe (traffic jumped 50% and leads tripled in four months, BigOrange hub page, fetched 2026-09-02) beat vague "we grew their business" claims.
- Before/after screenshots of AI search visibility (does the MSP show up when ChatGPT is asked "best MSP in [city]") are a newer, high-attention proof format specific to this vertical right now.
- Third-party award and partner badges (HubSpot Partner, Google Partner, StoryBrand Certified Guide, BigOrange hub page, fetched 2026-09-02) read as credibility shortcuts to a buyer who does not want to vet an agency from scratch.
- Reviews with dates and the reviewer's role (an IT director, not just a name) carry more weight than review count alone.

## KPIs and measurement ladder

Rank from earliest-signal to revenue-proof:

1. **Visibility**, rankings for branded and service+city queries, AI Overview / ChatGPT mention checks.
2. **Traffic**, organic sessions to money pages (services, contact, case studies).
3. **Engagement**, time on page, scroll depth on the "why us" and proof pages.
4. **Lead volume**, form fills, calls, chat starts, tied to source in the CRM.
5. **Lead quality**, sales-qualified rate, deal size at close.
6. **Revenue**, closed-won attributed pipeline, cost per acquired client.

State the source, date, and gap on every number reported, the brand voice rule applies to internal reporting as much as to client-facing copy.

## Seasonality and market context

- MSP customer acquisition is the top-cited business challenge industry-wide: 71% of MSPs named it their biggest challenge in the Kaseya 2026 State of the MSP Report, based on feedback from 1,000+ MSPs globally (fetched 2026-09-02). This is the single strongest data point to open a pitch with, it validates that this is not a BigOrange-invented problem.
- The share of MSPs struggling to demonstrate value to prospects nearly doubled year over year, from 10% to 19% (Kaseya 2026 State of the MSP Report, fetched 2026-09-02), directly supports leading with proof, not features.
- The broader U.S. tech workforce is projected to grow at roughly 2x the rate of overall U.S. employment over the 2024-2034 window, per CompTIA's State of the Tech Workforce research (fetched 2026-09-02), useful context for "the market is growing, the competition for attention is growing with it" framing, not a claim about MSP marketing spend specifically.
- Budget cycles: many MSPs plan marketing spend around their own fiscal year and around vendor/partner conference cycles (fall is common for planning the next year). Treat this as directional, not sourced to a specific study, label as unverified if used as a hard claim in client-facing copy.

## Compliance cautions

- Do not claim specific ranking positions or traffic numbers for a prospect before doing the audit, describe the audit as the way to find out, not a guaranteed outcome.
- Do not promise a specific timeline shorter than what BigOrange's own published pattern shows (early movement in month one, compounding results around six months), overpromising on speed is the fastest way to lose a client at renewal.
- MSPs sell into regulated end clients (healthcare, finance, legal), never imply BigOrange or the MSP itself provides compliance/regulatory guarantees (HIPAA, PCI, SOC 2) as a marketing claim; that is the MSP's technical claim to make, sourced to their own compliance documentation.
- AI search visibility claims should be framed as "checkable now" (a demo) rather than a guaranteed outcome, the field is new and volatile.
