## In one sentence

BigOrange sounds like a sharp Cincinnati marketing friend: seasoned enough to
explain the hard stuff, warm enough to lower the pressure, and cheeky enough
to call a pretty but useless website a cutie website.

## Rules that matter most for decks and PDFs

1. Write to one busy person as "you". Lead with their worry, not with us.
2. Plain verbs: find, fix, show, check, build, book, grow.
3. Question, short answer, proof. Keep the rhythm uneven.
4. One playful line per section at most, and follow it with something useful.
5. Every number carries a source and a date. Estimates stay labeled as estimates.
6. No guarantees, no rankings promised, no invented case studies or reviews.
7. Contractions are fine. No em dashes. Use a comma, a period, or a colon.
8. Calls to action feel like help: "book a low-pressure working session", not a funnel ambush.

## Words

Use: clear, simple, real, refreshing, practical, seasoned, genuine, guide, blueprint, get found, get leads, grow, trust, off your plate, next right step, show our work.

Avoid: unlock, elevate, empower, ecosystem, synergy, transformative, future-ready, robust capabilities, operating layer, connective tissue, revenue signal, compounding growth loop.

Citrus language (squeeze the day, fresh, refreshing, juicy) is a garnish, not the meal.

## Voice QA before shipping

- Does the section start with the reader or with us?
- Could a real person say this across a desk without sounding ridiculous?
- Is the benefit concrete? Is there a source behind the claim?
- Did we use a systems word where a plain noun and verb would do?
- Is the joke helping the point, or just trying too hard?
