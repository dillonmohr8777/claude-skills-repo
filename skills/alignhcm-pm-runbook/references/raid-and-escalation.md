# RAID, escalation, and go/no-go

## RAID log

Mandated on every project. It captures Risks, Actions, Information items, and
Decisions that affect the project, is shared with the client and the project
team, and tracks open items through transition and closure.

The RAID log is the source for section 5 of the weekly status report. An item
that is open in RAID but absent from the status report is a reporting error, not
a judgement call. The status report generator enforces the corollary: it will
not emit a green status while a high-severity RAID item is open.

Build the register with the client during readiness rather than after the first
problem. Maintain it with the client project lead, not alone.

## Escalation

**Internal:** project manager, then practice lead, then COO.

**External on UKG paper:** PM to PDAM, then practice lead, then the partner
alliance manager.

Flag a client as an internal escalation on the project Smartsheet and record the
problem, the impact, and the potential resolution. Bring it to the Monday PM and
practice lead forum.

## Weekly rhythm

| Day | What |
|---|---|
| Monday | PM and PL check-in on at-risk projects. Approve project team timesheets |
| Tuesday | Programme manager notifies anyone who did not submit time |
| Day before the client call | Internal team meeting: scope, budget, schedule, risks, RAID, plan, upcoming tasks |
| Client call day | Weekly status call with the customer and project team, 30 to 45 minutes |
| Within 24 hours | Issue the status report |
| Before Friday | Update the project status Smartsheet and add the weekly health report |

Health reports carry a colour and a comment for each of schedule, budget, scope,
and client. Schedule comments say on track or behind, and if behind, the plan to
catch up. Budget comments carry estimate at completion and burn rate. Scope
comments carry any change requests. Client comments carry engagement and
satisfaction.

## Status call structure

Nine points, in order. The status report mirrors these section for section.

1. Project status: green, yellow, or red
2. Attendance
3. Go-live dates
4. Key milestones and accomplishments
5. RAID log link
6. Project plan link, and upcoming deliverables
7. Discussion points
8. Action items
9. Other, including later phases

Discussion points cover Pro and Dimensions status, data conversion status,
Payment Services, integration files, key decisions and client feedback, risks to
go-live, training progress, and change management activities.

## Go / no-go readiness

One week before the first punch date, schedule a one-hour internal go/no-go
meeting. Capture and update the go-live readiness agenda, and upload the
completed sheet to the project console.

## Common risks

Scope creep. Data conversion problems, whether poor quality, hard to obtain,
difficult to map, or insufficiently validated. Banking setup. Interfaces.
Insufficient contingency time. Slipping timeline. Limited resource availability.

The counter to all of them is the same: set expectations up front for
requirements gathering, data conversion, and testing; identify risks with the
customer during readiness; keep the register live; and engage client leadership
early rather than at the point of failure.

## Closure

Submit the post-go-live intake form after the first payroll is processed. Submit
the support transition case seven days before the close of the second payroll.
Review open items from the RAID log. Schedule the transition call. Then complete
the closure checklist, capture lessons learned, archive, and remove Smartsheet
sharing.
